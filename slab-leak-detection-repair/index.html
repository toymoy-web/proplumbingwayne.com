Warm Spot on the Floor or Water Under the Floorboards?

A slab leak rarely announces itself with an obvious puddle. Most people notice a warm patch of floor over a hot water line, a section of flooring that stays inexplicably damp, or a water bill that jumped without an obvious cause. That's the specific problem slab leak detection and repair is built to solve: confirming exactly where a line is leaking underneath a concrete floor, then fixing it without tearing up more of the slab than necessary.

Homes built on a concrete slab foundation, common in parts of Wayne's newer construction, run hot and cold water supply lines directly beneath the floor. Over enough years, those lines can corrode, shift slightly with the foundation, or develop a pinhole leak at a joint, and because the leak is buried under a finished floor, it's usually the symptoms above the slab, not the leak itself, that give it away.

Plumbing work on a slab leak follows the same Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) principles that govern any buried water line repair in New Jersey, which is why proper detection before cutting matters: code-compliant repair means fixing the actual failure point, not guessing at a location and hoping.

Signs Something's Wrong

Signs of a Leak Under the Slab

  • ✓
    A warm spot on the floor, usually over a leaking hot water line.
  • ✓
    Water coming up between floorboards or tile grout lines.
  • ✓
    Low water pressure in one part of the house only.
  • ✓
    The sound of running water under the floor with everything shut off.
  • ✓
    Mold or a musty smell near baseboards with no other visible source.

Repair Options

How We Fix a Confirmed Slab Leak

Spot Repair

  • Targeted cut directly over the confirmed leak point
  • Less disruptive when the leak is isolated
  • Fastest option for a single, clearly located leak

Reroute Above Slab

  • Avoids cutting the concrete at all
  • Better option for older, corroding pipe runs likely to leak again elsewhere
  • Often preferred when the slab has multiple aging lines

We'll walk you through which option actually fits your situation once the leak is confirmed and located, rather than defaulting to the more invasive option by habit. The right call often depends as much on the age and condition of the rest of the slab's plumbing as it does on the single leak we've been called out for.
