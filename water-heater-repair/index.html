Signs Something's Wrong

Signs Your Water Heater Needs Attention

  • ✓
    No hot water at all, often a failed heating element, igniter, or gas control valve.
  • ✓
    Lukewarm water that runs out fast, a sign the tank can no longer hold its full temperature or capacity.
  • ✓
    Rust-colored water from the hot tap, which usually means the tank itself is corroding from the inside.
  • ✓
    Rumbling or popping noises, typically sediment buildup hardening at the bottom of the tank.
  • ✓
    Water pooling at the base of the tank, a sign of a failing tank or a loose fitting.
Repair or Replace?

When Repair Makes Sense vs. Replacement

Likely a Repair

  • Tank is under 8-10 years old
  • Single failed part: element, thermostat, or igniter
  • No visible rust or tank corrosion

Worth Discussing Replacement

  • Tank is over 10-12 years old
  • Rust-colored water or visible tank corrosion
  • Repeated repairs in the past year

A standard 40-50 gallon tank swap is usually a same-day job when the unit is in stock. A switch from tank to tankless takes longer since it often involves new gas or electrical line sizing work, and we'll walk you through that real difference before you decide anything.

Tank vs. Tankless

Choosing Between Tank and Tankless

Tank Water Heaters

Lower upfront cost and a straightforward swap in most cases, since it usually reuses the existing gas or electrical connection.

Tankless Water Heaters

Continuous hot water and a smaller footprint, but often needs upgraded gas line sizing or electrical capacity depending on the model.

Fuel Type

We service and install both gas and electric units. Switching fuel types on a replacement is possible but adds to the scope of the job.

Capacity

Household size and simultaneous hot-water demand (multiple showers, laundry) determine what gallon rating or tankless flow rate actually fits.
