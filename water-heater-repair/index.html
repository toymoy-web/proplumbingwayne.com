Signs Something's Wrong

Signs Your Water Heater Needs Attention

  • ✓
    No hot water at all, often a failed heating element, igniter, or gas control valve.
  • ✓
    Lukewarm water that runs out fast, a sign the tank can no longer hold its full temperature or capacity.
  • ✓
    Rust-colored water from the hot tap, which usually means the tank itself is corroding from the inside.
  • ✓
    Rumbling or popping noises, typically sediment buildup hardening at the bottom of the tank.
  • ✓
    Water pooling at the base of the tank, a sign of a failing tank or a loose fitting.
Repair or Replace?

When Repair Makes Sense vs. Replacement

Likely a Repair

  • Tank is under 8-10 years old
  • Single failed part: element, thermostat, or igniter
  • No visible rust or tank corrosion

Worth Discussing Replacement

  • Tank is over 10-12 years old
  • Rust-colored water or visible tank corrosion
  • Repeated repairs in the past year

A standard 40-50 gallon tank swap is usually a same-day job when the unit is in stock. A switch from tank to tankless takes longer since it often involves new gas or electrical line sizing work, and we'll walk you through that real difference before you decide anything.

Tank vs. Tankless

Choosing Between Tank and Tankless

Tank Water Heaters

Lower upfront cost and a straightforward swap in most cases, since it usually reuses the existing gas or electrical connection.

Tankless Water Heaters

Continuous hot water and a smaller footprint, but often needs upgraded gas line sizing or electrical capacity depending on the model.

Fuel Type

We service and install both gas and electric units. Switching fuel types on a replacement is possible but adds to the scope of the job.

Capacity

Household size and simultaneous hot-water demand (multiple showers, laundry) determine what gallon rating or tankless flow rate actually fits.

Leaking Tank

Water Around the Heater: What Is Leaking and What It Means

A puddle at the base of a water heater always gets attention, but the source decides what happens next. Some leaks are loose fittings that a technician can fix in one visit. Others mean the tank has corroded through. Looking closely at where the water starts tells you which kind you have.

  • ✓
    Pipe connections on top. Drips here are often a worn or loose fitting and may be repairable.
  • ✓
    The relief valve and its discharge pipe. Water here can mean pressure or temperature is running too high, which needs a technician.
  • ✓
    The drain valve near the bottom. A slow drip can come from a worn valve.
  • ✓
    The tank body or underside. Water from the shell means the inside has corroded through, and the tank needs replacing.

A tank near the fifteen year mark that leaks from the body is rarely worth repairing. Rust inside the tank cannot be reversed, and a patch buys very little time. We tell you whether you are facing a repair or a replacement before work begins, and the price is flat and written.

While you wait for us, close the cold water supply valve above the tank if you can reach it safely. Power off an electric unit at the breaker. Move anything stored near the heater away from the floor, especially in a finished basement.

Tankless Service

Flushing and Descaling a Tankless Water Heater

Tankless units heat water as it passes through a narrow heat exchanger. Mineral scale slowly coats that surface and restricts the flow. A unit that worked well for years can start to struggle without any failed part.

  • ✓
    Water that swings between hot and lukewarm during one shower.
  • ✓
    An error code on the display, or a unit that shuts off partway through use.
  • ✓
    Weaker hot flow while cold flow stays normal.
  • ✓
    Rattling or kettle sounds coming from the heater.

A service visit flushes the heat exchanger with a descaling solution through the service valves. Manufacturers commonly suggest a yearly flush, and homes with hard water may need it sooner. White crust on faucets is a good hint that scale builds quickly at your address. Installing service valves on a unit that lacks them can make later flushes quicker, which is worth raising on the call.

Each brand has its own flush procedure and error codes. Tell dispatch the make and model when you call so the right approach is planned. Hybrid heat pump units use different parts, so give us the model number and we will confirm what we can service before the visit.

Cold Showers

No Hot Water at All? Two Quick Checks Before You Call

Start with the power or the pilot

An electric unit can lose heat because a breaker tripped or a reset button popped. A gas unit may have lost its pilot. If a breaker trips again right away, leave it off and call us. If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call your gas utility first.

Check more than one tap

If only one faucet runs cold, the problem is probably in that fixture and not the heater. When every hot tap runs cold, the heater is the likely cause.
