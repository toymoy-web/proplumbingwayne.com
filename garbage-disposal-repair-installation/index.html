A Common Mistake

Why Resetting It Yourself Sometimes Isn't Enough

The red reset button on the bottom of most disposals handles a simple overload trip, but it won't fix a genuinely jammed flywheel or a failed motor, and repeatedly pressing it without addressing the actual jam can burn out the motor faster. If the unit still hums or does nothing after one reset attempt and a manual flywheel turn with the hex key, that's the point to call rather than keep trying.

Disposal units also have a real service life, typically 8-12 years depending on usage, and a unit nearing that age that starts developing multiple small issues at once is often more cost-effective to replace than to keep repairing piecemeal.

What goes down the disposal matters too. Fibrous vegetables, coffee grounds, and grease are common culprits behind premature wear, even though the unit will grind them without immediately jamming, since they build up on the flywheel and blades over time rather than causing an obvious single failure right away.

Repair or Replace?

When Repair Makes Sense vs. Replacement

Likely a Repair

  • Jammed flywheel or tripped reset
  • Leak at the sink or drain connection
  • Unit is under 5-7 years old

Worth Discussing Replacement

  • Leak from the unit body itself
  • Failed motor
  • Unit is over 8-10 years old
