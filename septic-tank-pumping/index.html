Why It Matters Here

Septic in a Mostly Sewered Town

Most of Wayne is on municipal sewer, but some homes, particularly on larger or more rural lots away from the denser neighborhoods, still rely on a septic system. Because septic service is less common here than in a fully rural area, it's easy for a system to go unmaintained simply because it's not top of mind the way it would be in a septic-only community. A tank that's gone more than five years without pumping is worth checking, even if nothing seems wrong yet.

Neglected septic systems don't just fail quietly. An overloaded tank can back up into the house through the lowest drains, or the drain field itself can become saturated and stop absorbing effluent properly, a much more expensive problem to fix than routine pumping would have been.

Our Process

What Septic Pumping Involves

Tank Location & Access

We locate the tank and access lid, sometimes requiring minor excavation if it's not marked.

Pumping

Solid and liquid waste is fully removed from the tank using a vacuum truck.

Inspection

We check the tank's condition and baffles while it's accessible, flagging any issues found.

Documentation

You get a record of the service date, useful for tracking your maintenance schedule going forward.

Maintenance

Keeping a Septic System Healthy Between Pumpings

What goes down the drain matters as much as the pumping schedule itself. Grease, so-called "flushable" wipes, and excessive garbage disposal use all add to the solid waste load faster than a tank is designed to handle, shortening the real interval between pumpings even on a well-maintained system. Spacing out laundry loads rather than running several in a row also helps avoid overwhelming the drain field with a sudden volume spike.
