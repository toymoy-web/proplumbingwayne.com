Catching It Early

Frozen vs. Already Burst

A frozen pipe that hasn't split yet is a very different, much less urgent job than one that has already burst. No water, or just a slow trickle, from one specific faucet during a cold snap, combined with visible frost on an exposed section of pipe, usually means it's frozen but still intact. That's the window worth calling in, before the expanding ice pressure splits the pipe wall and turns a thawing job into a repair and cleanup job.

Where It Happens

Which Pipes Freeze in Wayne Homes

  • ✓
    Pipes in an unheated crawlspace, especially in older homes without modern insulation.
  • ✓
    Supply lines against an exterior wall, particularly on the north side of the house.
  • ✓
    Outdoor spigot lines that weren't shut off and drained before the first hard freeze.
  • ✓
    Attic pipe runs near an uninsulated section of roof.

Safe Thawing

How We Thaw a Frozen Pipe Safely

Locate the Freeze Point

We trace which section is frozen rather than guessing at the whole pipe run.

Controlled Heat

Electric heat tape or a controlled heat source, never an open flame, which risks fire and pipe damage.

Pressure Check

Once thawed, we check for a split before considering the line clear, since ice can crack a pipe without an obvious leak until pressure returns.

Prevention

We flag the specific pipe run for insulation or heat tape so it doesn't freeze again at the next cold snap.

Why It Happens Here

Wayne's Real Winter Climate

Northern New Jersey sees genuine hard-freeze stretches most winters, not just the occasional cold night, and the pipes that fail are almost always the ones least protected from that reality: a crawlspace with a gap in the insulation, a supply line run too close to an exterior wall during original construction, or a spigot line someone forgot to shut off before the first freeze. None of these are unusual mistakes. They're just easy to overlook until the specific cold spell that finally exposes them.

The International Plumbing Code's freeze-protection guidelines call for water piping in unheated spaces to be insulated or otherwise protected, which is the same standard New Jersey's adopted plumbing code follows. A pipe that freezes in the same spot every winter usually means that protection was never installed correctly in the first place.

Safety First

Before We Arrive

  • ✓
    Never use an open flame or propane torch to thaw a pipe yourself. It's a real fire hazard, especially near wood framing in a crawlspace or wall cavity.
  • ✓
    A hair dryer on a visible, accessible section of pipe is a reasonable stopgap while waiting for us, but avoid it on a pipe you can't fully see.
  • ✓
    If you find standing water near a frozen section, that likely means it already split. Shut off the main water valve and call us right away.
  • ✓
    Keep the faucet nearest the frozen section slightly open if it's safe to do so, which relieves pressure as the ice begins to melt.
