Faucet Types

Why the Fix Depends on the Faucet Type

A dripping compression faucet, common in older Wayne homes, usually needs a simple washer replacement. Newer cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc faucets fail differently, and using the wrong repair approach on the wrong mechanism often makes the leak worse rather than better. That's why we identify the actual faucet type before quoting a fix, rather than assuming every drip has the same cause.

Kitchen faucets with a pull-down sprayer add another common failure point: the internal hose and its diverter valve wear out independently of the main faucet body, which is why a kitchen faucet can develop a new leak or lose spray function even after the main valve was already replaced once.

Bathroom sink faucets tend to fail differently, more often from mineral buildup inside the aerator or valve restricting flow than from an outright leak, which is why a sudden drop in pressure at just one sink is worth checking before assuming it's a bigger plumbing issue. According to plumbing code standards followed across New Jersey, supply line and shutoff valve work should isolate each fixture independently, which is what makes an under-sink repair possible without shutting off water to the whole house.

What We Check

How We Diagnose a Faucet or Sink Issue

Faucet Internals

Washers, O-rings, and cartridges wear differently by faucet type, and we match the fix to the actual mechanism.

Supply Lines

A failing flexible supply line connection is a common, easy-to-miss cause of under-sink pooling.

P-Trap & Drain Assembly

A worn seal or crack here mimics a faucet leak but needs a different fix entirely.

Water Pressure

Low pressure at one fixture only usually points to a clogged aerator or a partially closed shutoff valve, not a house-wide issue.
