Between Two Rivers

A Borough With Water on Both Sides

Lincoln Park was carved out of Pequannock Township in 1922, and the area was once known as Two Bridges and later Beavertown. The borough contains the easternmost point in Morris County, which sits along the Pompton River.

With the Pompton and Passaic Rivers on either side, groundwater and storm runoff are part of daily life here, and sump pumps do a lot of the work.

Before We Arrive

  • ✓
    Shut the main valve as soon as you know a pipe has failed.
  • ✓
    Cut power to the affected area at the breaker, never with wet hands or from standing water.
  • ✓
    Lift valuables off the floor and clear a path if the situation is safe.
  • ✓
    Phone our emergency line and tell us what you see so we can send the right equipment.

According to standard plumbing emergency response guidelines, shutting off water and power first protects both the house and the people in it. For homes close to either river, a quick call during a storm is better than waiting to see if the pump keeps up.

Around the Borough

Main Street, the Lake, and the Airport

Lincoln Park has a small local airport, a community lake that opens each summer, and a Main Street lined with older homes and newer apartment buildings. The borough has roughly 11,000 residents. That mix of older houses and newer buildings means the calls we take range from aging cast-iron drains to newer fixtures that simply need a skilled install.

Storm Season

Why Sump Pump Reliability Matters Here

A sump pump that works in dry weather can still fail the night a major storm arrives, because switches stick, float arms jam, and power drops out. We test the pump, the float, and the discharge line, and we recommend battery backup for basements that have taken on water before. If you are already dealing with water, call first and we will talk you through the safe steps.
