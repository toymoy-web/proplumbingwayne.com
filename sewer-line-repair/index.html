Why It Happens Here

Sewer Line Failure in Older Wayne Homes

Homes built before the 1970s near Packanack Lake and Preakness often still run clay or cast-iron sewer laterals, and both materials develop the same real problem over decades: cracks and joint separations that let tree roots in. Once inside, roots grow into a genuine obstruction, not a simple clog, and repeated snaking or jetting only clears a path through it temporarily rather than fixing the actual structural issue.

Pipe bellying is the other common failure mode in older lines: a section of pipe settles slightly and creates a low spot that collects waste and paper even when the rest of the line is clear. A camera inspection is the only reliable way to tell root intrusion, bellying, and simple age-related deterioration apart, since they can produce very similar symptoms above ground. According to the plumbing code standards New Jersey has adopted, sewer lateral repairs must maintain proper slope and material compatibility, which is part of why matching the repair to the existing pipe matters as much as fixing the immediate break.

Repair Options

How We Repair a Damaged Sewer Line

Spot Repair

  • Targets just the confirmed damaged section
  • Less excavation when the problem is localized
  • Fastest option for a single crack or joint failure

Trenchless Rehabilitation

  • Rehabilitates the line from the inside with minimal digging
  • Better suited to a longer run with multiple weak points
  • Depends on the pipe's overall condition and access

A camera inspection tells us which option actually fits, rather than defaulting to full replacement whenever a repair might do the job. Full replacement is reserved for lines with extensive, multi-point damage where a targeted fix genuinely wouldn't hold up.
