Why It Matters Here

Real Flood Risk in Wayne

Wayne Township's own emergency management guidance points to a documented history of flooding in low-lying areas along the Passaic River, and specifically recommends backflow valves on sewer lines for homes in those areas. A sump pump handles a different, related risk: groundwater and storm runoff rising into the basement itself, rather than sewage backing up through the drains.

Older homes near Packanack Lake and Pompton Plains with stone or block foundations are especially prone to this kind of water intrusion, since those foundations predate modern drainage standards. A working sump pump with a properly routed discharge line is one of the most cost-effective safeguards available for that specific risk.

Signs Something's Wrong

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Attention

  • ✓
    The pump runs constantly even without recent rain, often a sign of a stuck float switch or a genuinely rising water table.
  • ✓
    It doesn't turn on during a storm, which could mean a dead pump, a tripped breaker, or a jammed float.
  • ✓
    Unusual grinding or rattling noises, usually a sign of a failing motor or debris in the pit.
  • ✓
    Visible rust or age on a unit over 7-10 years old, which is close to the typical service life for a standard pump.

Why Power Loss Is the Real Risk

Standard Pump

Works reliably as long as the power stays on, which is often exactly the problem during a bad storm.

Battery Backup

Keeps the pump running through a power outage, precisely when flooding risk from a storm is often at its highest.

Water-Powered Backup

Maintenance

Keeping a Sump Pump Reliable

A sump pump that's never tested is the one most likely to fail exactly when it's needed. We recommend a simple annual check before storm season: pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the pump activates, runs quietly, and shuts off cleanly once the water level drops. If it hesitates, runs longer than usual, or doesn't kick on at all, that's worth a service call before the next real storm rather than after.
