A Different Problem Than the Leak

Plumbing Repair vs. Water Damage Restoration

If a pipe just burst or a sump pump just failed, the pipe or pump itself is a plumbing repair, handled on our main line. Water damage restoration is what happens next: extracting standing water, drying out wet drywall, insulation, and flooring, and preventing the mold growth that follows once materials stay damp for more than a day or two. These are genuinely different trades with different equipment, which is why this service runs on its own dedicated response line.

The scale of the job also matters here. A small spill is a cleanup task; a burst pipe that ran unnoticed overnight or a sump pump failure during a multi-day storm is a structural drying job involving equipment most homeowners don't own and wouldn't want to rent for a one-time event. Renting that equipment yourself for a single incident often costs close to what professional service runs, without the experience to know when materials are actually dry versus just dry on the surface.

What We Do

The Restoration Process

Structural Drying

Why Speed Matters

The First 24 to 48 Hours

Mold spores are present in virtually every home already, and they need very little to start growing: damp material and roughly 24 to 48 hours of standing moisture is often enough. That window is why we treat water damage calls as time-sensitive, not something to schedule for later in the week. The faster standing water is extracted and drying begins, the less likely you are to end up needing separate mold remediation on top of the water damage itself.

According to industry guidelines followed across the restoration trade, materials that stay wet longer than 48 hours are generally treated as a higher mold risk requiring more aggressive drying and, in some cases, removal of porous materials like drywall or carpet padding that can't be fully dried in place.
