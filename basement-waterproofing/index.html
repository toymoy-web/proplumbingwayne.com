Why It Matters Here

Real Foundation Risk in Wayne

Older homes near Packanack Lake and Pompton Plains with stone or block foundations were built before modern drainage standards existed, and they're genuinely more prone to water intrusion than newer poured-concrete foundations. Combined with the clay-heavy soil common across parts of Passaic County, which drains slowly and holds water against the foundation longer after a storm, these homes see a real, recurring seepage pattern rather than an occasional fluke.

The fix that actually works for this specific combination of foundation type and soil is almost always an interior drainage system rather than exterior sealants alone, since sealing the outside of a stone foundation doesn't stop water from finding its way through the porous material and mortar joints over time. An exterior fix also means excavating around the entire foundation, which is far more disruptive and costly than working from inside the basement.

Signs Something's Wrong

Signs You Need Waterproofing

  • ✓
    A musty smell in the basement that doesn't go away with ventilation.
  • ✓
    Damp or discolored walls, especially at the cove joint where the wall meets the floor.
  • ✓
    Efflorescence, a white, chalky mineral deposit left behind as water evaporates through the foundation.
  • ✓
    Visible seepage after heavy rain, even if it dries up within a day or two.

Our Approach

How We Waterproof a Basement

Source Diagnosis

We confirm whether water is coming through the foundation wall, the cove joint, or a specific crack before recommending a fix.

Interior Drainage

A channel along the foundation's interior perimeter collects water and routes it to a sump pit, the standard approach for older stone and block foundations.

Crack Repair

Active cracks get sealed from the inside with a method built to hold under real hydrostatic pressure, not a surface patch.

Sump Pump Integration

Waterproofing and a properly sized sump pump work together, not as separate, unrelated fixes. Water collected by the drainage channel needs somewhere reliable to go, and that's the pump's job.
