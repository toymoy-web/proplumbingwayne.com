If This Sounds Familiar

Line Keeps Clogging Again After Snaking?

A lot of the calls we get for hydro jetting start the same way: a line was snaked, it drained fine for a few weeks, and then it clogged again. That pattern usually means the cable cleared a path through the blockage without removing what actually caused it, whether that's grease buildup lining a kitchen line or tree roots that have grown back into a sewer joint. Hydro jetting solves that specific problem by scouring the entire pipe wall rather than just punching a hole through.

Grease & Roots

The Two Most Common Reasons a Line Needs Jetting

Kitchen lines build up grease slowly, one meal at a time, until the pipe's usable diameter has shrunk enough that even normal use backs up. A cable pushes through that buildup without removing it, which is why the same kitchen line often clogs again within weeks of a standard snaking.

Sewer laterals face a different problem: tree roots find their way into the smallest crack or joint in an older clay or cast-iron pipe and grow into a real obstruction over time. Jetting cuts through root intrusion far more thoroughly than a cable, though a line with serious root damage sometimes needs a camera-confirmed repair alongside the jetting rather than jetting alone.

Fit Check

Which Lines Suit Jetting and Which Need Repair First

Jetting works well on buildup. It does not fix a broken pipe. A camera look before the job separates the two cases, which protects you from paying for a cleaning that a damaged line cannot hold.

Good candidates

Kitchen branch lines coated with grease, main lines slowed by scale, and laundry or floor drain lines that clog on a regular schedule.

Roots in a sound pipe

Root growth through a joint can often be cut back, followed by a camera pass to confirm what remains.

Repair first

A cracked, collapsed, or badly sagging section holds water and debris no matter how clean the pipe walls are.

Fragile old pipe

Very brittle lines are inspected before any high pressure is used, and we tell you if the pipe is not ready.

Smells and Slow Flow

When Sewage Odor or a Slow Main Line Points to Buildup

A slow main line with a faint sewer smell often means the pipe walls are lined with grease and scale. Waste moves slowly, settles, and produces odor. Cleaning the pipe wall can bring flow and smell back to normal when buildup is the only cause.

If the smell stays after jetting, look for other causes. A dry trap, a failed vent, or a cracked drain can all let sewer gas into the house, and a post-jet camera pass helps rule them out.
