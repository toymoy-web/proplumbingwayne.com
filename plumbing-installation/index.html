Permits

Permits & Inspection

New Jersey plumbing work generally requires a permit and inspection from Wayne Township, and skipping that step creates a real problem later, particularly when selling the home and a buyer's inspector finds unpermitted work. We handle the permit application and coordinate the required inspection as part of the installation, rather than leaving that step for you to navigate separately.

Beyond the paperwork, permitted work also means the installation is verified by an independent inspector before it's closed up behind a wall or under a floor, catching a mistake while it's still cheap and easy to fix rather than after everything is finished.

Planning

What to Think About Before We Start

Fixture Locations

Moving a sink or toilet even a few feet can mean rerouting supply and drain lines, which affects both cost and timeline.

Existing Line Capacity

Adding a new fixture sometimes means checking whether the existing drain and vent lines can handle the added load.

Timeline Coordination

Plumbing rough-in needs to happen before drywall goes up, so scheduling around your contractor's timeline matters.

Fixture Sourcing

We can install fixtures you've already purchased or help source them, whichever fits your project and timeline best.

After Installation

Testing Before We Consider It Done

Every new installation gets pressure-tested and run through multiple normal-use cycles before we call the job complete, not just a visual check that water comes out where it's supposed to. Catching a slow leak at a new connection during our own testing is far better than you discovering it a week later once fixtures are in place and flooring is finished.

Aging Homes

Installing Into Wayne's Older Housing Stock

A new fixture installation in an older Wayne home sometimes surfaces issues the original construction didn't anticipate: galvanized supply lines that need a transition fitting to modern PEX or copper, or a drain configuration that predates current venting requirements. We flag these when they come up rather than forcing a new install onto incompatible existing plumbing, since doing it right the first time avoids a callback down the road.
